Gmail Mail Merge — Step by Step

Send one personalised email to a whole list, straight from Gmail. No add-ons, no code.

Before you start

Mail merge sends a separate, personalised email to each person on a list. Nobody sees anyone else's address, and replies come back to your inbox as normal conversations.

You need three things:

  1. Gmail in a desktop browser at mail.google.com. The phone app cannot do this.
  2. Your work Google account — not a personal @gmail.com one.
  3. A Google Sheet with your list. We build it in Step 1.

First run takes about 10 minutes. After that it is 2 minutes per campaign.

You can send to 1,500 recipients per day, counted across everything you send that day.

Step 1 — Build the Google Sheet

Open a new Google Sheet and type your list. Row 1 must be the column headers — Gmail turns each header into a merge tag you can drop into the email.

A working sample:

EmailFirst NameSchoolTopic
derek.chan@plkps.edu.hkDerekPLK Primary SchoolGoogle Workspace for Education renewal
mary.wong@stpaul.edu.hkMarySt Paul's Collegemoving your school mail to Gmail
kk.lee@tsuenwan.edu.hkKKTsuen Wan Secondaryclassroom collaboration tools

Rules that matter:

  • One column must hold the email addresses. Name it Email so it is obvious.
  • Headers can be anything — First Name, School, Topic, Meeting Date. Spaces are fine.
  • No blank rows in the middle of the list. Gmail stops reading at the first empty row.
  • Keep each cell short. It gets dropped into a sentence, so moving your school mail to Gmail reads well, MAIL MIGRATION PROJECT does not.
  • Fill every cell if you can. Blanks leave gaps unless you set a default value (Step 4).

Step 2 — Turn on Mail merge in Gmail

The toggle sits in the To row of a new message — not in the Send button.

  1. In Gmail, click Compose.
  2. Click the full-screen icon at the top right of the compose box. In a narrow window the toggle is hidden.
  3. Click inside the To field. The Cc and Bcc labels appear.
  4. At the far right of that same row, switch on Mail merge.
┌─ New Message ───────────────────────────────── ⌄ ⤢ ✕ ─┐
│ To                           ( ●─) Mail merge  Cc  Bcc │  ← here
│ Subject                                                │
│                                                        │
│ [ Send ▾ ]   ← "Schedule send" lives here, not the merge │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

When it is on, the To field changes colour and a Sheets icon appears.

If you cannot see the toggle

  • The compose window is full screen (step 2 above).
  • The From address is your own — not an alias, and you are not writing as someone else's delegate.
  • No scheduled send is set on this draft, and Confidential mode is off. Both block mail merge.
  • You are on desktop Gmail, not the mobile app or offline mode.

Still nothing? Your account's Google Workspace plan does not include the feature. Only an IT admin can change that — no setting on your side will bring it back.

Step 3 — Pull the list in from your Sheet

  1. Click the Sheets icon in the To field (or the Add from a spreadsheet link).
  2. Pick the Sheet you saved in Step 1.
  3. Gmail asks which column holds the email addresses — choose Email.
  4. Click Insert.

The To field now shows how many recipients were loaded, for example 3 recipients. Check that number against your Sheet. If it is short, you almost certainly have a blank row in the middle of the list.

Every other column — First Name, School, Topic — is now available as a merge tag.

Step 4 — Write the email with merge tags

Merge tags work in both the subject line and the body. To insert one, type @ and pick a column from the dropdown that appears. It drops in as a small chip.

Write this, using the sample Sheet from Step 1:

Subject: @First Name, a question about @School

Hi @First Name,

I work with a number of Hong Kong schools on @Topic, and I thought it might be worth a short chat with @School.

Would 20 minutes next week suit you? Happy to work around your timetable.

Best regards,
[your name and signature]

Derek receives:

Subject: Derek, a question about PLK Primary School

Hi Derek,

I work with a number of Hong Kong schools on Google Workspace for Education renewal, and I thought it might be worth a short chat with PLK Primary School.

Mary receives the same email with her own name, school and topic. Each person gets a private message of their own.

Set a default for blank cells

If a cell is empty the tag sends as nothing, and Hi , looks careless. To protect against it:

  1. Click the merge tag chip you inserted.
  2. Enter a default value, for example there for @First Name.

Anyone with a blank cell then gets Hi there, instead of a gap. Do this for every tag you use.

Step 5 — Test, then send

Always test first. There is no preview and no undo: the moment you click Send, every email goes out for real.

  1. Add yourself as an extra row in the Sheet, with realistic values in every column.
  2. Re-insert the list in Gmail so your row is included.
  3. Click Send. Gmail shows a confirmation with the recipient count — read it before confirming.
  4. Open the email that arrives in your own inbox. Check the name, the school, the topic and the subject line all read naturally.
  5. Fix anything odd, delete your test row, re-insert the list, and send to the real group.

What each recipient gets:

  • A private email addressed only to them. Nobody can see the rest of the list.
  • Your normal signature and formatting.
  • An unsubscribe link at the bottom. Gmail adds this automatically and it cannot be removed.

Replies land in your inbox as ordinary emails. If someone unsubscribes, Gmail leaves them out of your future merges.

What it cannot do

Merge tags insert plain text only. That rules out a few things people often ask for:

You wantPossible?
Different name, school, date or sentence per personYes — any column works
The same image for everyoneYes — insert it in the body as usual
A different image per person, from a URL columnNo — the tag sends the URL as text
A different link target per personNo — same reason
Attachments that differ per personNo
Cc or BccNo — both are disabled in merge mode
Scheduling the sendNo — scheduling and merge cannot be combined
Removing the unsubscribe footerNo

If you need per-person images, per-person links or attachments, Gmail is the wrong tool — that needs a script-based merge instead. Ask Tommy.

Quick troubleshooting

  • Toggle missing — see the checklist at the end of Step 2.
  • Fewer recipients than expected — a blank row in the middle of the Sheet.
  • Tag dropdown does not appear when typing @ — you have not inserted the Sheet yet. Do Step 3 first.
  • Email arrives with gaps, e.g. Hi , — blank cells with no default value set. See Step 4.
  • Send button refuses — you are over the 1,500 recipients for today. Split the list across two days.
  • Tag shows as literal text like @First Name — you typed it by hand instead of picking it from the dropdown. Delete it and re-insert with @.
